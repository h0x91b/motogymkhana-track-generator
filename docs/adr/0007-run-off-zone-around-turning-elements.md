# Turning elements keep a 5 m run-off zone of free asphalt

A rider who goes wide in a U-turn or a loop needs somewhere to recover. The organiser's field rule is about 5 m of empty asphalt round any cone taken by a turn. So every turning element (u-turn, gate turn, mirror, keyhole, crescent — "turnarounds"; pivot turn, figure eight, box, circle, snail, box eight — "loops") reserves its core grown by 5 m: on every side for a loop, on every side but the entry side for a turnaround, whose entry side is the rider's own straight lead-in. The run-off zone must lie on the site (the 1 m margin counts), and no other element's zone and no start or finish line other than the element's own may be in it. Connectors may cross it and run-off zones may overlap, because both are just asphalt ridden one rider at a time.

Tall cones at the centre of connector U-turns are placed objects too and stay out of every run-off zone.

The cost is real and accepted. Measured at v13 on top of cruiser-sized U-turns (ADR 0006) and connector U-turn cones, mean elements placed of 8, cruiser, 100 seeds: 40×60 hard closed train 4.92 → 4.59, 30×45 hard closed 2.88 → 2.28, 60×80 hard open unchanged. Tight closed sites need the looser closing-connector limit more often (1.7% → 4.8% of closed courses in the invariant set). The soft-criteria thresholds were lowered to match.

## Considered Options

- 3–4 m instead of 5 m — wins back only about a third of the loss; the cost is mostly structural (inside the site, neighbours kept out), so the organiser's figure was kept.
- Letting the run-off zone hang over the site edge — recovers about half of the loss, but the run-off would be off the asphalt, which defeats its purpose.
- Leaving the entry side of loops free too — no measurable gain.

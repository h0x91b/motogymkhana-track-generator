# A lone cone is turned round by as much as the route needs

Amends ADR 0027 and ADR 0030. A tester's 40 × 52 course has «Конус справа» at (33, 5), 2.35 m east of and 2 m above the entry of a slalom ridden westwards, under a crescent whose line comes down to y 8. Straight past the cone, the way into the slalom loops off the lot; a 180° small U-turn centred on the cone needs its way in 2r above the slalom's line, through the crescent. On the asphalt the rider comes in at a slant, keeps the cone on its side and rounds it by whatever angle puts him onto the slalom. The rule an organiser gives for a passed cone is only the side: it neither forces nor forbids a turn.

**One concept: a turn round a lone cone.** «Конус слева», «Конус справа» and «Малый разворот» are each an arc round their own cone, turning towards it, on a circle that holds the cone at least `passOffset` (WEAVE, 1 m, or the clearance) inside: the cone stays on its side all the way round, the rider picks the line. Each type has a range and a preferred turn (`CONE_TURNS`): a passed cone 0–225°, preferring 0° (the point beside the cone of ADR 0027); a small U-turn 135–225°, preferring 180° centred on the cone (ADR 0030). The circle's radius is the small U-turn's own, and `SMALL_TURN_RADIUS_MIN` (3 m) round a passed cone on any bike: a passed cone has no size, and a turn round it is the organiser's, who put the cone where only a tight turn reaches the next element. The circle's centre is free; the entry and exit headings follow from it.

**The preferred turn first, the others only when it breaks a rule.** `freeHeadings` gives the FREE_TRIES best headings of the preferred turn as before, then (`coneTurns`) every other turn, shortest first by the Dubins path in, the arc and the Dubins path out: on each of the sixteen axes a turn by each CONE_TURN_STEP (22.5°) multiple, passing a passed cone at the offset mid-arc or centred on a small U-turn's cone; a turn ending on the line straight into the next entry, or up to 1 m beside it within CHAIN_ANGLE, slid back along it in 0.5 m steps and entered on each axis (the exit heading is what the next element needs); and one starting on the line straight out of the previous exit, slid along it, by each turn. A turned passed cone chains ahead like the small U-turn (ADR 0030), so the turn's exit is joined straight to the next entry. The arc alone is the element (no run-in or run-out): the connectors meet it at its ends, as at a point.

**`routeLayout` turns; the generator keeps the preferred turn.** A course in «Правка» tries the other turns where the preferred one fails, and a course file keeps only the elements: the route finds the same turn again. The generator never uses them: a tighter turn is the organiser's choice (ADR 0030), and trying them for every failed filler slows generation for little gain (below). A generated course routes back to itself as before, since the preferred turn keeps every rule there and is tried first in both.

**What the card says.** A turned passed cone: «Один высокий конус, красный: объезд с разворотом ~N° направо, конус справа»; a turned small U-turn: «Разворот вокруг … высокого конуса на ~N° (конус справа)», N to 5°. The cone keeps the colour of its side: a turn past 180° is not a pivot turn's striped cone. The plan, 3D and the PNG draw the arc as the element's own line.

Measured with `scripts/bench.mjs` (seeds 1–100): every generated course is unchanged, so the elements placed are too; the tester's course as saved routes with no problem on connectors 7 → 8 and 8 → 9; routing it takes about 3.5 ms instead of 1.

## Considered Options

- A free angle for the small U-turn only (the first brief): the tester's element is «Конус справа» and must stay one; two concepts for a lone cone would differ only in the range.
- An arc centred on the cone: its exit at the radius beside the cone never lines up with an entry 3.1 m from the cone, as the slalom's is; the centre has to move.
- Connectors on 3 m round a passed cone: would turn tighter than the class anywhere near one, which ADR 0006 exists to prevent; the turn belongs to the element, where the cone is the only thing on the asphalt.
- Let the generator use the other turns too: on the benchmark recipes (seeds 1–100) it places 0.03–0.10 more elements of 5–8 at two and a half to four times the time per course, and puts 3 m turns on courses whose organiser asked for none.
- A continuous angle and centre (optimised per pair of neighbours): exact, but the router's "first that keeps every rule in a fixed order" needs a finite, ordered set, and 22.5° steps plus a slide along the next element's way in cover what a rider does.
